Outpost is compiled automatically once a day. This page explains exactly how, so you can judge how far to trust it.

1. Sources

The briefing reads about 30 public RSS and Atom feeds: the security press and vendor research blogs (for example Krebs on Security, BleepingComputer, The Record, Unit 42, Microsoft Security and Google Threat Intelligence), defence press and analysts (Breaking Defense, Defense News, The War Zone, War on the Rocks, Bellingcat), and geopolitics, space and navigation outlets (Atlantic Council, Foreign Policy, The Diplomat, SpaceNews, Inside GNSS, BBC, Al Jazeera, DW). For vulnerabilities it uses the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue and the NIST National Vulnerability Database. The complete list is in config/sources.json. Vendor blogs report their own research, so read them with that in mind.

2. Collection

  • Time window: only items published in the last 36 hours are kept.
  • Topic filter: general news outlets and defence press are filtered by keyword, so only items about security, defence technology, space and geopolitics get in. Everything else is dropped.
  • Copied, not rewritten: headlines, links, dates and outlet names are taken straight from the feeds.
  • Failures are shown: if a feed does not respond, it is skipped and listed on the Today & Learn page.

3. Ranking

Stories are ranked by recency, a weight for the source, and how many different outlets cover the same story. Near-identical headlines from different outlets are merged into one entry that lists every outlet. Nobody can pay for inclusion or rank. The Cylist card sits outside the ranking and is labelled.

4. Summaries and the role of AI

  • A language model writes each short summary and “why it matters” line. The model used each day is named on the Today & Learn page.
  • It is given only the headline and the first few hundred characters of the outlet’s own snippet. It has no web access while writing.
  • It is told not to add facts. As a safeguard, any number written in digits in its text that does not appear in the source text causes that summary to be discarded, and the outlet’s own snippet is shown instead.
  • Links and outlet names are never written by the model.
  • The daily checklist and the “Learn today” concepts are also model-written from the day’s headlines. Treat the checklist as a prompt for your own judgement, not as security advice.

This guard catches invented figures. It cannot catch every misreading, so summaries can still be wrong.

5. What Outpost does not do

  • It does not verify events itself. It summarises and links other outlets’ reporting.
  • It does not publish opinion or predictions of its own.
  • It does not give financial, investment, legal or operational advice.

6. Illustrations

The diagrams beside each story are generated by code from the story’s subject. They are decorative, not photographs or maps of the event. Photographs, where used, are credited on the About page.

7. Limits

Summaries are pointers, not substitutes for the source: always read the linked report before acting. Early reports of breaches and military events are often revised, and a ranked list can miss stories its sources did not carry.

8. Open source

The pipeline, ranking rules and source list are public at github.com/kyrillidis/outpostdaily under the MIT licence. Corrections and source suggestions are welcome as issues or pull requests.
